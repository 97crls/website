The 2026 robot · REBUILT™

One piece at a time

00 / 08
  1. Frame1-inch aluminum extrusion chassis with gussets and mounts. Everything else bolts to this.
  2. Swerve modulesFour SDS MK4i modules under covers. Each one drives and steers on its own, so the robot can strafe while it turns.
  3. ElectronicsThe electronics panel: power distribution panel, robot radio, an Orange Pi coprocessor, and the battery.
  4. HopperExtrusion uprights and brackets that form the hopper and hold the fuel.
  5. IndexerThe bottom indexer and five compliant star churros that keep fuel moving toward the shooter.
  6. IntakeTen 2.25-inch compliant wheels on hinging polycarbonate side panels pull fuel off the floor.
  7. ShooterThe hood assembly, driven by NEO motors through MAXPlanetary and VersaPlanetary gearboxes, plus the Arducam camera mount.
  8. BumpersRed or blue depending on the alliance, with 97 on every side.

Latest · March 2026

Rising All-Stars

At the New England District North Shore Event, the judges gave Team 97 the Rising All-Star Award. It recognizes a team that's growing fast, and it came one year after our 2025 run to the finals at URI.

A few weeks later at the WPI District Event, our robot was part of a 308-point alliance, the highest score we put up all season. The plaque went home to the trophy case the team cleaned out and set up this spring.

What we do

One robot, four crews

Students run every part of the team. You don't need experience to start. Most of us didn't have any.

01

Mechanical

CAD, cutting, drilling and assembly. Aluminum, polycarbonate and a lot of rivets.

02

Electrical

Motor controllers, sensors and power distribution, wired clean enough to survive a match.

03

Programming

Java code for driver controls and autonomous routines. We'll teach you from zero.

04

Strategy & Ops

Scouting, drive team, fundraising, social media and running the pit at events.
